UNDER THE MAP

Data, assumptions & limitations

Real inputs

  • 2021 Koriyama paddy parcel boundaries: MAFF Fude polygons, transformed and distributed by AIGID. Kind = 田. These are agricultural parcel approximations, not surveyed property boundaries or a 2019 rice census.
  • Sentinel-1 RTC imagery: before 7 October 2019, 05:43 JST; after 13 October 2019, 05:42 JST. Descending orbit 46. Radar backgrounds show actual VV imagery on the selected date. The before-event image does not establish zero flooding or normal water depth.
  • Flood predictions: the unchanged project Random Forest using eleven pre/post VV/VH features, threshold 0.05. Independent Japanese flood accuracy has not been established.

Alignment and imagery

Original JGD2011 field coordinates are transformed to WGS84 for Leaflet. No manual offset is applied. Source-to-display rounding is below 0.1 m; radar crops share the same grid and values. A known local parcel-to-imagery offset was reproduced near 140.32648 E, 37.38094 N. A newer 2026 official parcel layer retained the mismatch. Field-level overlap and yield scenarios remain approximate; no alignment correction is claimed. Parcels are 2021 approximations; the aerial mosaic has varying capture dates and the flood radar is from 2019. Use zero field fill to inspect local differences.

How each field is measured

Flood overlap is the share of valid 10 m radar pixel centres inside a parcel classified as flooded. Flooded hectares = polygon area × this share. Zero sample centres means unknown. Small parcels and mixed boundary pixels can be unreliable. Any positive sample marks “flood detected”.

Fields are selected by centroid within the earlier Koriyama study rectangle. All stats cover the selected parcels, not the whole city. Parcel boundaries have changed since the 2016 coarse paddy grid used earlier, so these area totals differ from that earlier result.

Three analysis stages

Before: real 7 October radar, 2021 paddy-map proxy and assumed normal harvest potential. Flood extent: native 10 m classifier cells across all land in the locked study rectangle, shown without field outlines. Area totals count source pixels; the display uses nearest-neighbour reprojection. This is retrospective model detection using both 7 and 13 October imagery, not a forecast issued before the flood. Harvest impact: hypothetical production remaining and lost under the assumptions, not observed post-flood crop condition. Baseline standing potential = parcel ha × assumed yield × assumed unharvested share. Remaining potential subtracts scenario loss. Remaining standing rice per hectare divides remaining tonnes by total parcel hectares; it is not yield per harvested hectare. Reduction uses the same parcel-area denominator. Production lost shows tonnes per field. Unknown flood samples remain unknown in impact; the study total excludes these parcels from both standing-potential and remaining-potential accounting.

The loss calculation is a scenario

Loss (t) = predicted flooded ha × normal yield (t/ha) × unharvested fraction × damage fraction

Damage fractions are published mean Random Forest lookup predictions from Bill-Weilandt et al. (2026), Table S11, maturity stage. This dashboard uses those exact three lookup nodes; it does not execute or retrain the original crop-damage RF. The model was developed in Thailand and Myanmar and has not been validated for this Japanese event.

Depth, duration, growth stage, yield, rice planting and harvest status are assumptions. The yield uses an illustrative paddy/rough-rice mass basis. No quality downgrade, contamination or price loss is estimated. Already harvested rice gives zero standing-crop loss. Classifier scores are not calibrated flood probabilities.

The scenarios are not confidence intervals or measured losses. Flood and crop-map errors are not included in the loss totals. The model cannot infer crop health, flood depth or duration from the before/after pair alone.
